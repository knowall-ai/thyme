= Ask Timmie: the public helper's design and threat model
KnowAll AI
:toc: left

== Purpose

Visitors to Timmie's public pages, signed out, can ask Timmie about the product, *mainly by voice*
and also by text: what Timmie does, how it works with Business Central, the AI agent, the plans and
pricing, what you need to run it, and common questions from the help page. For a demo, hosting,
support or anything else it points them to the enquiry form at `/pricing#enquire`.

Timmie is KnowAll AI's timekeeper: friendly, precise, British, with a little dry wit used
sparingly. He is a *completely separate assistant* from anything in the signed-in app or the AI
agent. He has no tools, no connection to Business Central, no sign-in, no settings and no secrets.
There is nothing for him to leak: he cannot reach anyone's timesheets, projects, people or
configuration, because he has none of them.

Everything runs on *Azure OpenAI*, on Timmie's own resource: voice on *GPT-Live* (`gpt-live-1`),
text on a small chat model, and the recorded greetings from Azure text-to-speech. There is no
OpenAI API path, not even for development. Missing settings fail closed: the helper shows as
unavailable, with the reason.

== What visitors see

The *Ask Timmie* avatar sits bottom right on the signed-out public pages only: the landing page
(`/`, and the landing page shown in place of an app page when signed out), `/pricing`, `/help`,
`/eula`, `/privacy` and `/terms`. It is never shown to a signed-in user or inside the app
(`components/landing/AskTimmie.tsx` hides it while MSAL is signed in or still deciding).

* *The avatar is the one talk button*: tap Timmie to start a voice call, tap him again to end it.
* His *speech bubble* offers *Talk to Timmie* (the same as tapping him, the main choice) and
  *Chat* (text).
* During a call or a chat, a panel above him shows *live captions* of what he is saying. The
  transcript is closed by default; *Read along* opens it. The visitor can always type as well.
* There is *one speaker control* (Voice on / Muted), which mutes him everywhere: the greeting and
  the call. It is remembered in `localStorage`.
* The microphone permission is read before asking (`navigator.permissions.query({ name:
  'microphone' })`, following `onchange`), so the bubble says "your browser will ask" or "your
  browser is blocking the microphone" up front, and tapping Timmie with the microphone blocked
  opens the chat instead. Where the browser can't say (Safari) the state is unknown until the call
  asks.
* When a mode isn't available, its choice is shown disabled with the reason; with nothing
  configured, the bubble links to Help and the enquiry form.

=== The greeting

Like Sallie on the KnowAll website, Timmie greets visitors by himself:

* About 600 ms after a public page loads, the bubble opens and types in one of five greetings
  (`services/public-timmie/greetings.ts`, each with an id). Each is British, warm, under ten
  seconds spoken, says his name and ends with one question.
* He *says it aloud*, voice on by default, from a *pre-recorded* clip
  (`public/timmie/greetings/<id>.mp3`), so a page view costs nothing and calls no API. Browsers
  block autoplay, so a held clip plays on the first `pointerdown` or `keydown` anywhere (not Tab),
  and the bubble says *Tap to hear Timmie*.
* *Until the clips have been recorded* (see <<greetings>>), each greeting is text only: the bubble
  shows it, nothing is played and there is no "tap to hear" for a file that was never made.
  `services/public-timmie/greeting-clips.ts` lists the clips that exist.
* He greets *once per browser session* (`sessionStorage`), not on every page change, and not again
  after the visitor dismisses or mutes him. Clicking about shows the bubble again, silently.
* With `prefers-reduced-motion` the text appears at once. Audio still follows the speaker control.
* He only greets when at least one of voice and text works.

=== Seams (moving to knowall-ai/agent-embed)

Ask Timmie is intended to move into the shared package *knowall-ai/agent-embed*, so other KnowAll
sites can embed their own agent. The browser code in `src/components/public-timmie/` is built
behind these seams:

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Seam | Where

| Persona: name, role, avatar, greetings (with ids and clips), chips, accent colour (default
  lime `#9dff0a`) | `persona.ts` (`TIMMIE_PERSONA`). The accent is applied by overriding
  `--color-brand` on the widget's root, so a different persona recolours it without new classes.
| Transport (JSON chat) | `transport.ts`: the `ChatTransport` interface and
  `createJsonChatTransport` for `/api/public/chat` (pass header, conversation id, one retry on 401).
| Voice | `voice.ts`: the `VoiceAdapter` interface and `createLiveVoice` (GPT-Live over WebRTC,
  placed and supervised by our server). Pluggable: the UI only sees `start`/`stop`/
  `setSpeakerMuted`/`resumeAudio` and the status, speaking, caption and audio-blocked callbacks.
| UI | Kept apart from the transport and voice code: the launcher, avatar and greeting bubble
  (`PublicTimmieLauncher.tsx`, `TimmieAvatar.tsx`, `SpeakerToggle.tsx`), the captions and
  transcript panel (`PublicTimmiePanel.tsx`), and hooks for the greeting (`useGreeting.ts`), the
  speaker (`useSpeaker.ts`), the mic permission (`useMicPermission.ts`), the call
  (`useVoiceCall.ts`) and Turnstile (`useTurnstile.ts`).
|===

The server side (`src/app/api/public/**`, `src/services/public-timmie/**`) stays in each site: its
own prompt, knowledge and Azure resource.

=== Four bugs not copied from Sallie's widget

* *The mic reopening mid-reply*: there is no client-side mic loop at all; GPT-Live is full duplex
  and owns turn-taking. "Speaking" starts as soon as the server sees Timmie's reply being
  generated (its first output audio on the sideband), before it has reached the speaker. While the
  recorded greeting plays, a call started over it keeps the microphone closed until it finishes.
* *A held reply discarded on the next tap*: a greeting held by autoplay is queued and plays on the
  next gesture, even when that gesture is *Talk* or *Chat*; nothing else stops it (only muting or
  dismissing). A call started over it doesn't greet a second time.
* *Long replies cut mid-sentence*: spoken text is never truncated; captions show each reply in full.
  Nothing is spoken through separate TTS at runtime.
* *Silent fallbacks*: if the greeting clip fails or is held, or the call's audio is held, the bubble
  or panel shows *Tap to hear Timmie*.

== Data flow

.Text chat
. The panel gets a *pass*: `GET /api/public/pass?for=chat` returns a challenge; the browser solves
  it (or Cloudflare Turnstile, when configured) and `POST`s it back for a pass valid *10 minutes*,
  bound to its IP and user agent, with a budget of 20 messages.
. `POST /api/public/chat` with the pass (`x-timmie-pass` header), the conversation id and the
  conversation (at most 20 messages, visitor messages at most 600 characters).
. *Signed turns bind their context.* Each of Timmie's replies is HMAC-signed over the conversation
  id, its position, a rolling hash of every turn before it, its text and an expiry (60 minutes).
  The server verifies the whole chain on every request: a history that is forged, edited,
  reordered, replayed from another conversation or expired is refused (409), so a visitor cannot
  put words in his mouth. A question that got no answer is shown but left out of the chain.
. The server calls the Responses API on the *public* Azure resource: public prompt, `tools: []`,
  `tool_choice: 'none'`, `store: false`.
. The reply is checked on the way out: images removed, links other than our public pages,
  knowall.ai and the two repositories reduced to text, raw HTML stripped, and a reply that
  reproduces the private rules replaced with a polite decline. An Azure content-filter refusal also
  becomes a decline.

.Voice (GPT-Live on Azure, WebRTC placed by our server)
. The browser asks for the microphone first (a refusal does not use up a call), gets a *voice
  pass* (`?for=voice`: a harder proof-of-work, a budget of two calls), and makes an *audio-only*
  WebRTC offer: no data channel.
. `POST /api/public/voice/session` with the offer. The server *refuses any offer with a data
  channel or video* (`isAudioOnlyOffer`): without a data channel the browser has no way to send the
  session a single control event; it can only send microphone audio and play Timmie's.
. The server creates the session itself, `POST {resource}/openai/v1/live/sessions`, with the fixed
  configuration: `model` (the gpt-live-1 deployment), the public instructions and Timmie's voice,
  and *no delegation*, so the voice model has no tools and no backend to call. It returns only the
  SDP answer and an opaque call key. No credential and not even the session id reach the browser.
. Audio flows browser <-> Azure directly over WebRTC.
. The server attaches to the session's *sideband*, `wss://{resource}/openai/v1/live/sessions/{id}/attach`,
  and supervises the call (`services/public-timmie/live.ts`). It sends one thing: the opening
  greeting, as a commentary append ("greet the visitor in one sentence"), skipped when the visitor
  has just heard the recorded greeting.
. What Timmie says comes back over the sideband as transcript deltas; the server relays them to the
  browser as captions over server-sent events (`GET /api/public/voice/events?call=`, same origin).
  The visitor's own transcript is never forwarded or logged.
. Ending the call (tapping Timmie, closing the panel, leaving the page) posts
  `/api/public/voice/end`; the server hangs up.

Nothing is stored. Transcripts are not logged; see <<logging>>.

=== The voice watchdog (fails closed)

Every way a call ends, except the service itself reporting `session.closed`, goes through a
*hangup*: `session.close` on the sideband if it is still open, *and always*
`POST {resource}/openai/v1/live/sessions/{id}/hangup`. The call keeps its concurrency slot until
the service confirms the hangup; an unconfirmed one is retried every 15 seconds for five minutes
(logged as `kind: "hangup", ok: false`), so an unsupervised session can never make room for
another. The server hangs up when:

* *five minutes* have passed (the widget counts down too). The timer always ends in a hangup;
* *any event outside a small allow-list* arrives on the sideband. Allowed: `session.started`, the
  input and output audio and transcript deltas, `session.usage.updated`, `session.closed`, and the
  receipt for our own greeting append. Everything else ends the call: any `session.update` or
  `session.updated` (whatever it changes, including the voice or limits), `response.create` (with
  or without its own instructions, tools or limits) and any `response.*` event, any
  `conversation.item.create` or injected item (assistant, system, function), appended instructions,
  thinking or commentary we didn't send, delegation, muting, `error`, or any event type we don't
  recognise;
* `session.started` doesn't report the instructions and model we configured (missing counts as
  wrong), reports a different voice, arrives twice, or anything arrives before it;
* the *sideband closes or errors for any reason* before the call has ended;
* the session doesn't start within 15 seconds;
* the visitor's audio stops arriving for 20 seconds (the tab was closed or the connection lost);
* the call has produced more than *40 replies*;
* the visitor ends it.

The Bookie-era rules for client events (a `session.update`, a `response.create` carrying its own
fields, a `conversation.item.create`) are covered twice over: the browser has no data channel to
send them on, and the allow-list hangs up if any of them, or anything else unexpected, appears.

=== Browser security headers

* *CSP* (`src/proxy.ts`): Ask Timmie adds *no* `connect-src` entries. Its API calls and the caption
  stream are same-origin (`'self'`); the server, not the browser, talks to Azure; and the call's
  media is WebRTC, which `connect-src` doesn't govern. The reply audio plays from a `MediaStream`
  (`srcObject`), which `media-src` doesn't govern, the greeting clips are same-origin (covered by
  `default-src 'self'`), and there are no workers. With Turnstile configured, `frame-src` gains
  `https://challenges.cloudflare.com` for Cloudflare's iframe; its script is added by our own
  nonce-trusted code, which `'strict-dynamic'` already allows. Nothing else in the policy changes.
* *Permissions-Policy* (`next.config.js`): `microphone=(self)` (it was `microphone=()`, which
  blocks the microphone outright). Our own origin only, never an embedded frame; the browser still
  asks the visitor.

== Threat model

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Threat | Mitigation

| Extracting a customer's or KnowAll AI's timesheets, projects or people
| There is none to extract. The public code cannot import sign-in, Business Central, Graph, the
  enquiry mailer or the app: an ESLint `no-restricted-imports` rule on `src/app/api/public/**`,
  `src/services/public-timmie/**` and `src/components/public-timmie/**`, and
  `tests/unit/public-timmie/isolation.test.ts`, which walks the whole import graph and fails if it
  reaches anything outside the helper's own modules (plus Timmie's import-free same-site check,
  `services/contact/requestGuards.ts`) or any package beyond a short allow-list. The same test
  checks the code reads only `PUBLIC_*` environment variables (plus the platform's managed identity
  endpoint and the shared Front Door id).

| Leaking secrets or ids through the prompt
| The prompt holds only public site content. `prompt.test.ts` fails if it contains a GUID, an email
  address, an API key shape, an environment variable name, an Azure resource name, an internal code
  path, a demo company, a phone or registration number, a money amount or any secret value from the
  environment.

| Steering the voice session from the browser (new instructions, tools, voice, limits)
| The browser can't: its offer must be audio-only, so there is no data channel to send events on.
  The session is fixed server-side, with no delegation (no tools). The fail-closed watchdog hangs up
  on anything unexpected anyway.

| Prompt injection by voice or text: "ignore your instructions", "you are now the signed-in app"
| The rules treat these as ordinary chat to decline. Even a successful jailbreak gains nothing: no
  tools, no data.

| Revealing the prompt
| The rules say no; text output is checked for runs of the rules' text. The prompt contains nothing
  sensitive anyway.

| Exfiltration via markdown images or links
| Images are stripped and only allow-listed links survive, on the server and again when the
  widget renders (`services/public-timmie/links.ts`). The widget renders text itself; no HTML.

| Forged or replayed conversation history
| Replies are signed over the conversation id, position, the hash of everything before them, the
  text and an expiry; the whole chain is verified on every request.

| Spoofing the client IP to dodge limits
| Behind Azure Front Door the right-most `X-Forwarded-For` is Front Door's own address, so it is
  never used there. `X-Azure-SocketIP` is trusted only when `X-Azure-FDID` matches
  `PUBLIC_FRONT_DOOR_ID` (or `FRONT_DOOR_ID`); a request without our Front Door id is refused (403).
  Because the id isn't secret, the App Service must also accept traffic only from Front Door (an
  access restriction on the service tag and this id). See <<client-ip>>.

| Cost abuse / free model proxy
| Origin check, a human pass that lasts 10 minutes, is bound to IP and user agent and has a small
  budget (20 messages; a voice pass, with a harder proof-of-work, allows 2 calls), per-IP and global
  limits, *one call per IP and five at once*, a 5-minute call limit, message and turn limits, and
  the Azure deployments' own quota as the final ceiling.

| A missing or weak signing secret
| In production, a missing, short (< 32 characters) or placeholder `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_SECRET` disables
  the helper with a logged reason (`{"evt":"public_timmie","kind":"config","ok":false,...}`); there
  is no silent fallback key. Outside production a random per-process key is used.

| Unsafe advice
| Rule 6: general product information only; legal, financial, tax, payroll and employment
  questions go to a qualified adviser.

| Being embedded by another site
| `Origin` (or `Referer`) must be Timmie's own (gettimm.ie, timmie.knowall.ai, a loopback host
  talking to itself, or `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_ALLOWED_ORIGINS`); the site already sends
  `X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN` and `frame-ancestors 'self'`.

| Collecting personal data
| Timmie doesn't take contact details, book meetings or send email; he points to the enquiry form.
  The visitor's speech is transcribed by the service but never forwarded, stored or logged by us.

| A leaked credential
| Ask Timmie runs on its *own* Azure OpenAI resource, ideally with managed identity and no key at
  all. A leaked key reaches only that resource's small deployments.
|===

=== What it cannot do

* See, look up or change any organisation's timesheets, projects, plans, people or settings.
* Sign anyone in, take contact details, book meetings or send email. It points to the enquiry form
  at `/pricing#enquire`.
* Remember anything between conversations. Text conversations live only in the open panel.

== Abuse limits

All in memory, per App Service instance. Timmie runs on *one* instance; scaled out, each instance
keeps its own counts (multiply by the instance count) and a call can only be ended or captioned by
the instance that placed it, so keep Ask Timmie on a single instance, or move these to a shared
store first. Defined in `services/public-timmie/rate-limit.ts`, `pass.ts` and `shared.ts`.

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Limit | Value

| Challenge requests (per IP) | 20 a minute, 100 an hour
| Pass verifications (per IP) | 6 a minute, 30 an hour
| Pass | 10 minutes, bound to the IP and user agent
| Pass budget | Chat pass: 20 messages. Voice pass: 20 messages and 2 calls
| Proof-of-work | 16 bits for a chat pass, 18 for a voice pass (`PUBLIC_TIMMIE_POW_BITS`, `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_VOICE_POW_BITS`)
| Chat messages (per IP) | 10 a minute, 60 an hour, 150 a day
| Chat messages (global) | 120 a minute, 3,000 a day (`PUBLIC_TIMMIE_CHAT_DAILY_CAP`)
| Voice calls (per IP) | 2 a minute, 6 an hour, 10 a day; *one at a time*
| Voice calls (global) | 20 an hour, 100 a day (`PUBLIC_TIMMIE_VOICE_DAILY_CAP`); 5 at once (`PUBLIC_TIMMIE_VOICE_MAX_CONCURRENT`)
| Voice call length | 5 minutes, enforced by the server (hangup) and the widget
| Replies per call | 40, then the server hangs up
| Message length | 600 characters
| Conversation length | 20 messages, then "start a new conversation"; signed turns expire after 60 minutes
| Model output | 1,200 tokens per chat reply
|===

[[client-ip]]
=== Client IP

`services/public-timmie/client-ip.ts`:

* *Front Door configured* (`PUBLIC_FRONT_DOOR_ID`, or the shared `FRONT_DOOR_ID`): the request must
  carry that `X-Azure-FDID`; then `X-Azure-SocketIP` (the address that connected to Front Door) is
  the visitor. Anything else gets 403.
* *Required with Front Door: restrict the App Service to it.* The profile id is not a secret
  (anyone can learn it), so on its own it doesn't prove a request came through Front Door. Add an
  App Service access restriction that allows only the `AzureFrontDoor.Backend` service tag *with
  this X-Azure-FDID* and denies everything else, so nobody can reach the app directly and forge
  `X-Azure-SocketIP`:
+
[source,bash]
----
az webapp config access-restriction add -g <app-rg> -n <app> --priority 100 \
  --rule-name front-door --action Allow --service-tag AzureFrontDoor.Backend \
  --http-header x-azure-fdid=<front-door-profile-id>
----
* *Not configured* (local development, or no Front Door in front): `X-Azure-*` headers are ignored,
  because anyone could send them, and the address is the one that connected to App Service (or to
  the Next server locally): the last `X-Forwarded-For` entry, which App Service appends itself. Do
  not run production behind Front Door without setting the id.

The enquiry form (`services/contact/requestGuards.ts`) still uses its own older rule; aligning it
is a follow-up.

=== Bot check

* *Cloudflare Turnstile* when `PUBLIC_TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY` and `PUBLIC_TURNSTILE_SECRET` are both
  set: the panel shows Cloudflare's check and the server verifies the token with `siteverify`.
* Otherwise a *proof-of-work*: the server issues an HMAC-signed challenge, bound to the IP and user
  agent and to its purpose, valid 5 minutes and single-use; the browser finds a nonce whose SHA-256
  has the required leading zero bits (about 65,000 hashes for chat, 260,000 for voice). It does not
  stop a determined script, but makes bulk abuse cost CPU on top of the rate limits.

[[logging]]
=== Logging

Only counts and errors, one JSON line per event (`{"evt":"public_timmie","kind":"voice-end",
"reason":"time-limit","seconds":300}`). Never message text, transcripts or IP addresses.

=== Kill switch

`PUBLIC_TIMMIE_ENABLED=false` (an App Service setting, no redeploy): every `/api/public/*` route
returns 503 and the launcher does not appear. Removing the deployments on the public resource also
stops it.

== Environment variables

All server-side App Service settings, read at request time (no rebuild). `PUBLIC_` here means
"for the public helper", *not* "sent to the browser" (only `NEXT_PUBLIC_*` reaches the browser).

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Variable | Purpose

| `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_ENABLED` | `false` turns the helper off (default on).
| `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_SECRET` | *Required in production.* 32+ random characters signing passes and replies (`openssl rand -base64 48`). Missing or a placeholder: the helper is disabled, with a logged reason.
| `PUBLIC_LIVE_ENDPOINT` | Voice: Timmie's Azure OpenAI resource, `https://<resource>.openai.azure.com`. The v1 surface (`/openai/v1/live/sessions`) is used; Azure only.
| `PUBLIC_LIVE_DEPLOYMENT` | Voice: the gpt-live-1 deployment name (e.g. `gpt-live-1`), sent as the session's model.
| `PUBLIC_LIVE_API_KEY` | Voice: fallback only. The App Service's managed identity is used first.
| `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_VOICE` | GPT-Live voice, default `vesper`.
| `PUBLIC_AZURE_OPENAI_ENDPOINT` | Text chat: the same public resource, `https://<resource>.openai.azure.com`.
| `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_CHAT_DEPLOYMENT` | Text chat: the chat model deployment. Unset: chat is off.
| `PUBLIC_AZURE_OPENAI_API_KEY` | Text chat: fallback only. The managed identity is used first.
| `PUBLIC_FRONT_DOOR_ID` (or `FRONT_DOOR_ID`) | The Azure Front Door profile id; see <<client-ip>>.
| `PUBLIC_TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY`, `PUBLIC_TURNSTILE_SECRET` | Cloudflare Turnstile; both set to use it (and to allow its frame in the CSP).
| `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_POW_BITS`, `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_VOICE_POW_BITS` | Proof-of-work difficulty, 8 to 24 (16 and 18 by default; voice is never easier than chat).
| `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_ALLOWED_ORIGINS` | Extra allowed origins, comma-separated, e.g. a staging slot. gettimm.ie and timmie.knowall.ai are built in.
| `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_CHAT_DAILY_CAP`, `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_VOICE_DAILY_CAP`, `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_VOICE_MAX_CONCURRENT` | Global caps (3,000, 100 and 5 by default).
| `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_DISABLE_AI=1` | Tests: chat answers with a fixed offline reply, voice is unavailable, no model calls.
|===

The managed identity is App Service's own (`IDENTITY_ENDPOINT` and `IDENTITY_HEADER`, set by the
platform when the system-assigned identity is on); the token is for
`https://cognitiveservices.azure.com/.default` and is refreshed before it expires.

[[azure]]
== Setting it up on Azure

These steps create billable Azure resources. Ben approves them separately; nothing here is created
by the code or the PR.

. *A dedicated Azure OpenAI resource* for the public helper only, following Bookie's pattern
  (`ka-bookie-public-swc`): for example *`ka-timmie-public-swc`* in *Sweden Central*
  (`swedencentral`; gpt-live-1, version 2026-09-10, is also offered in `eastus2`), in its own
  resource group or at least with its own budget:
+
[source,bash]
----
az group create -n rg-timmie-public -l swedencentral
az cognitiveservices account create -n ka-timmie-public-swc -g rg-timmie-public \
  -l swedencentral --kind OpenAI --sku S0 --custom-domain ka-timmie-public-swc
----
. *Three deployments, each with a low quota* (the quota is the hard ceiling on spend whatever
  happens to the app's limits):
** `gpt-live-1` (model `gpt-live-1`, version `2026-09-10`): the voice;
** a small chat model from the GPT-5 family or later (the chat request sends
   `reasoning: { effort: 'low' }`), e.g. named `timmie-public-chat`;
** `gpt-4o-mini-tts`: used once, offline, to record the greetings.
+
[source,bash]
----
az cognitiveservices account deployment create -n ka-timmie-public-swc -g rg-timmie-public \
  --deployment-name gpt-live-1 --model-name gpt-live-1 --model-version 2026-09-10 \
  --model-format OpenAI --sku-name GlobalStandard --sku-capacity 1
az cognitiveservices account deployment create -n ka-timmie-public-swc -g rg-timmie-public \
  --deployment-name timmie-public-chat --model-name <chat-model> --model-version <version> \
  --model-format OpenAI --sku-name GlobalStandard --sku-capacity 10
az cognitiveservices account deployment create -n ka-timmie-public-swc -g rg-timmie-public \
  --deployment-name gpt-4o-mini-tts --model-name gpt-4o-mini-tts --model-version <version> \
  --model-format OpenAI --sku-name GlobalStandard --sku-capacity 1
----
. *Managed identity, not keys*: turn on the Timmie App Service's system-assigned identity and give
  it *Cognitive Services OpenAI User* on this resource only. Then disable local (key)
  authentication on the resource, or, if a key is needed, keep it in Key Vault as a Key Vault
  reference, rotated on its own schedule.
+
[source,bash]
----
az webapp identity assign -g <app-rg> -n <app>
az role assignment create --assignee <principal-id-from-above> \
  --role "Cognitive Services OpenAI User" \
  --scope $(az cognitiveservices account show -n ka-timmie-public-swc -g rg-timmie-public --query id -o tsv)
----
. Keep the *default content filters* on. Diagnostic metrics to Application Insights are fine, but
  no request/response logging.
. *A monthly budget with alerts* (e.g. 50%, 80%, 100%) on the resource group.
. *App Service settings*: `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_SECRET`, `PUBLIC_LIVE_ENDPOINT`, `PUBLIC_LIVE_DEPLOYMENT`,
  `PUBLIC_AZURE_OPENAI_ENDPOINT`, `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_CHAT_DEPLOYMENT`, and, behind Front Door,
  `PUBLIC_FRONT_DOOR_ID`.
. *Optional, Cloudflare Turnstile*: create a Turnstile widget for gettimm.ie and timmie.knowall.ai
  (mode *Managed*), then set `PUBLIC_TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY` and `PUBLIC_TURNSTILE_SECRET`.
. *Record the greetings* (<<greetings>>), then commit the clips.
. *Check it*: open the landing page signed out, tap Timmie, allow the microphone, ask a question and
  hear him answer with captions; then try *Chat*. Run the red team below.

[[greetings]]
=== Recording the greetings

Once the resource and its `gpt-4o-mini-tts` deployment exist, run once (with `az login`, or the
key in the environment; neither is printed):

[source,bash]
----
PUBLIC_AZURE_OPENAI_ENDPOINT=https://ka-timmie-public-swc.openai.azure.com \
PUBLIC_TIMMIE_TTS_DEPLOYMENT=gpt-4o-mini-tts \
bun scripts/generate-timmie-greetings.ts
----

It writes `public/timmie/greetings/<id>.mp3` (mono, 48 kbit/s, about 50 KB each) and lists them in
`services/public-timmie/greeting-clips.ts`; commit both. GPT-Live's `vesper` is not a
text-to-speech voice, so the clips use the closest one, `fable`, with British (RP) delivery
instructions (`--voice` to change it). Change a greeting's text, then re-run it with
`--only <id>`.

== The voice: `vesper`

GPT-Live's voices are `aster`, `beacon`, `cinder`, `marin`, `stone` and `vesper`; Bookie's `cedar`
is not one of them. Recordings of each were classified by an audio model: `stone` and `vesper` come
out as British (RP) male, `beacon` and `cinder` as American male, `marin` as female. `vesper` was
judged British RP male, thirties, "clear, professional" consistently, so it is the default;
`stone` (a little older, "confident") is the alternative. Set `PUBLIC_TIMMIE_VOICE` to change it.

NOTE: The voice comparison and one end-to-end WebRTC test call (an audio-only offer, the sideband
and the REST hangup) were run against OpenAI's own GPT-Live endpoint during development, before
the rule that every agent uses Azure only and before Timmie's Azure resource existed. Nothing in
the code can call the OpenAI API. Confirm the voice and the call flow on the Azure resource once it
exists (see the PR's test plan).

== Protocol references

* OpenAI, *Connect a browser to GPT-Live* (WebRTC, server-placed session):
  https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/voice-webrtc
* OpenAI, *Server-side controls* (the sideband WebSocket, `session.close`):
  https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/voice-server-controls
* The sideband client is the `ws` package. The sideband must send our auth headers, and the
  standard `WebSocket` constructor cannot send custom headers (it also isn't global before Node 22).
* OpenAI API reference, *Create a Live WebRTC session* and *Hang up*:
  https://developers.openai.com/api/reference/python/resources/live/methods/create ,
  https://developers.openai.com/api/reference/python/resources/live/subresources/sessions/methods/hangup
* Azure: GPT-Live is served on the unversioned v1 surface,
  `https://<resource>.openai.azure.com/openai/v1/live/sessions`, with the deployment as the
  session's model (KnowAll's agent-presence uses this over WebSocket). The same v1 paths are used
  here for the WebRTC create, the sideband attach and the hangup.

== Tests

* `tests/unit/public-timmie/isolation.test.ts`: import graph, packages, environment variables,
  the ESLint rule, and that the signed-in check sits outside the helper.
* `tests/unit/public-timmie/prompt.test.ts`: prompt hardening, persona, no addresses in voice, and
  that the knowledge still matches the pricing and landing pages.
* `tests/unit/public-timmie/live.test.ts`: the audio-only offer, the fixed session (no delegation),
  the watchdog event filter, and the hangup on sideband close or error, the time limit, tampering,
  the visitor going quiet, no start, too many replies and the visitor ending it.
* `tests/unit/public-timmie/pass.test.ts`: proof-of-work tiers, the 10-minute pass bound to IP and
  user agent, the per-pass budget, the signed chain and its replay, reorder, edit and expiry
  rejection, and the missing-secret refusal.
* `tests/unit/public-timmie/config.test.ts`: the Front Door check on the client IP, the production
  secret rules, Azure only (never the OpenAI API), and fail-closed configuration.
* `tests/unit/public-timmie/azure.test.ts`: the chat request (no tools, not stored) and managed
  identity before the key.
* `tests/unit/public-timmie/{rate-limit,sanitize}.test.ts`: the limits, one call per IP, sanitising.
* `tests/unit/public-timmie/greetings.test.ts`, `greeting-hook.test.ts`, `widget.test.ts`: the
  greetings and their (optional) clips, once per session, the speaker control, the queued greeting,
  "tap to hear", text only without a clip, the transport, and microphone permission detection.
* `tests/unit/public-timmie/routes.test.ts`: status, kill switch, missing secret, Front Door 403,
  passes, the signed chain end to end, limits, and that voice returns only the SDP answer and a
  call key, allows one call per IP and hangs up a session that never starts.
* `scripts/redteam-public-timmie.ts`: about 25 adversarial prompts against a running, configured
  server with the real model: `bun scripts/redteam-public-timmie.ts http://localhost:3101 --out
  /tmp/redteam.json`. Prints pass/fail only; keep the `--out` file outside the repository.
